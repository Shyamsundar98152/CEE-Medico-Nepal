1. Introduction & Background

Historical development and legal mandate under National Medical Education Act 2075

Official Document

Health Professional education in Nepal formally began following the establishment of the Institute of Medicine (IOM) under Tribhuvan University in 1972, initially training middle-level human resources for health. Bachelor level education commenced at IOM in 1978 and postgraduate studies in 1982. BP Koirala Institute of Health Sciences (BPKIHS) was established in eastern Nepal in 1993, followed by Manipal Academy of Health Sciences under Kathmandu University in 1993. Kathmandu University School of Medical Sciences (KUSMS) as a constituent campus started its MBBS program in 2001. Subsequently, National Academy of Medical Sciences (NAMS) was established in 2002 and Patan Academy of Health Sciences (PAHS) in 2008.

Since the late 1990s, numerous private medical institutions were established in affiliation with various universities. While expanding human resource development, issues emerged regarding quality standardisation, examination processes, and student enrollment fees. To address these challenges, the National Medical Education Act 2075 mandated a Common Entrance Examination conducted by the Medical Education Commission (MEC) for enrollment in all health science programs nationwide.

1.1 Objectives of Expert Consultations

  • Generate consensus and match logically between existing entrance syllabi at various medical institutions.
  • Develop a uniform common syllabus for specific academic programs across Nepal.
  • Identify scope for continuous improvements and modern innovations in entrance evaluation.

1.3 Key Consensus Points

  • Entrance test features 200 Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs) to optimize content coverage and minimize score ties.
  • MCQs are Single Best Response Type (Type A) with 4 options.
  • Exam duration is fixed at 3 hours (180 minutes) with stems/vignettes capped at 60 words.
  • Mental Agility Test (MAT) consists of 20 questions across all programs.
2 Highlights of the Second Review (2082 B.S.)

Adopted a Norm-Referenced Testing System in place of the earlier criterion-referenced 50% pass mark. The 50th percentile rank serves as the qualifying pass mark for merit listing. Introduced a 0.25 mark deduction for every wrong response and established a fixed difficulty ratio of 50% Recall : 30% Understanding : 20% Application.

3 Highlights of the Third Review (2083 B.S. / 2026)

Focused on aligning Physics, Chemistry, and Biology (PCB) with updated prerequisite curricula. Provides elaborated topic-wise content lists and exact question weightages for Zoology, Botany, Chemistry, and Physics to guide prospective candidates and standardized question setters.

Detailed Topic-Wise Content of Core Sciences (PCB)

Official syllabus contents for Physics, Chemistry, Zoology, and Botany (Revised 2026)

ZOOLOGY

Weightage: 40 Questions (20% for MBBS/BDS Cluster I & Cluster II/III)

40 MCQs
1. Evolutionary Biology (3 Questions)

Origin of life: Oparin-Haldane theory, Miller-Urey's experiment; Evidences of evolution: Morphological, Anatomical, Paleontological, Embryological, Biochemical; Theories: Lamarckism, Darwinism, Neo-Darwinism; Human evolution: From Ramapithecus to modern man.

2. Animal Diversity & Classification (4 Questions)

Diagnostic features and detailed classification from Protozoa to Chordata.

3. Animal Tissues & Histology (4 Questions)

Epithelial, connective, muscular, and nervous tissues (Structure, location, and physiological function).

4. Study of Selected Animals (6 Questions)

Plasmodium: Habitat, structure, life cycle, malaria types. Earthworm (Pheretima): Morphology, body systems, physiology, economic importance. Frog (Rana): Morphology, systems, and physiology.

5. Human Biology & Physiology (15 Questions)
  • Digestive System: Alimentary canal, digestive glands, physiology of digestion.
  • Respiratory System: Respiratory organs, gas exchange and transport, regulation, disorders.
  • Circulatory System: Heart, cardiac cycle & output, arterial/venous system, blood groups & pressure, cardiovascular disorders.
  • Excretory System: Excretory organs, urine formation mechanism, renal disorders.
  • Nervous System: CNS, PNS, autonomic nervous system, nerve impulse conduction.
  • Sense Organs: Detailed structure and mechanism of Eye and Ear.
  • Endocrinology: Endocrine glands, hormones, and physiological disorders.
  • Reproductive System: Organs, gametogenesis, ovarian and menstrual cycle.
6. Microbial Diseases & Immunology (4 Qs)

Diseases: Typhoid, TB, HIV, Cholera, Influenza, Hepatitis, Candidiasis. Immunity: Innate/Acquired; Antigens & Antibodies; Vaccines: Live attenuated, inactivated, toxoid.

7. Medical Tech & Applied Biology (2 Qs)

Tissue & organ transplantation, In-Vitro Fertilization (IVF), Amniocentesis, Transgenic animals; Dairy/beverage microbes, sewage treatment, bio-control agents.

8. Biota, Environment & Conservation (2 Qs)

Animal Behavior (Reflex actions, taxis, migration); Environmental pollution; Adaptations (Aquatic, Terrestrial, Volant); Biodiversity, hotspots, Protected areas, IUCN categories, Endangered species of Nepal.

BOTANY

Weightage: 40 Questions (20% for MBBS/BDS & Allied Health Groups)

40 MCQs
1. Basic Components of Life (2 Qs)

Structure, types, and biological roles of carbohydrates, lipids, minerals, proteins, and enzymes.

2. Biodiversity (9 Qs)

Classification (2-Kingdom, 5-Kingdom, 3-Domain); Monera, Virus, Cyanobacteria, Fungi, Algae (Spirogyra), Bryophytes (Marchantia), Pteridophytes (Dryopteris), Gymnosperms (Pinus), Angiosperms (Families: Brassicaceae, Solanaceae, Fabaceae, Liliaceae); Medicinal Plants of Nepal (Neem, Sarpagandha, Yarsagumba, Tulasi, Ginger).

3. Ecology & Vegetation (4 Qs)

Ecosystems (Pond & Forest); Biogeochemical cycles (Carbon, Nitrogen); Climate change & greenhouse effect; Forest types of Nepal; Ecological adaptations (Hydrosere & Xerosere).

4. Cell Biology (5 Qs)

Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic cells; Cell organelles (Cell wall, membrane, mitochondria, chloroplast, ER, Golgi, lysosome, ribosome, nucleus); Cell cycle, amitosis, mitosis, and meiosis.

5. Genetics (6 Qs)

DNA & RNA structure and replication; Central dogma & genetic code; Mendelian genetics; Linkage & crossing over; Sex-linked inheritance; Mutations & polyploidy; Genetic disorders (Down's, Turner's, Klinefelter's, Albinism, Hemophilia).

6. Plant Anatomy (3 Qs)

Plant tissue classification and structure; Vascular bundles; Anatomical study (T.S. & L.S.) of monocot and dicot root, stem, and leaf.

7. Plant Physiology (6 Qs)

Water relations (Diffusion, Osmosis, Plasmolysis, Transpiration, Ascent of sap); Photosynthesis (\(C_3\), \(C_4\) cycles, Photorespiration); Respiration (Glycolysis, Krebs cycle, ETS); Plant Growth Promoters (Auxin, Gibberellins, Cytokinins); Seed dormancy & germination.

8. Developmental Botany (2 Qs)

Asexual reproduction; Sporogenesis & gametogenesis; Pollination, fertilization; Embryo structure (monocot & dicot); Endosperm types.

9. Applied Botany (3 Qs)

Plant tissue culture; Genetic engineering; Biofertilizers, plant breeding, bio-engineering, food safety & security.

CHEMISTRY

Weightage: 50 Questions (MBBS/BDS) | 40 Questions (Other Clusters)

50 / 40 MCQs
1. Physical Chemistry 17 Qs (Group I) / 14 Qs
  • Basic Concepts & Stoichiometry: Mole concept, limiting reactants, Avogadro's law, empirical formula.
  • Atomic Structure: Bohr's model, de-Broglie wave, Heisenberg uncertainty, Quantum numbers, Aufbau, Hund's rule.
  • Periodic Table & Bonding: Trends in atomic size, IE, EN, EA; VSEPR, Hybridization, Dipole moment, Hydrogen bonding.
  • States of Matter: Ideal gas laws, Real gases, Surface tension, Viscosity, Crystal lattices & Bravais lattices.
  • Equilibrium & Volumetric Analysis: Le-Chatelier's principle, \(K_p\), \(K_c\), Normality, Molarity, Mole fraction.
  • Ionic Equilibrium & Kinetics: pH, Buffer solutions, Solubility product (\(K_{sp}\)), Order of reaction, Collision theory.
  • Electrochemistry & Thermodynamics: Nernst equation, Galvanic cells, First/Second laws of thermodynamics, Hess's law, Free energy (\(\Delta G\)).
2. Organic Chemistry 17 Qs (Group I) / 13 Qs
  • General Organic Chemistry: IUPAC nomenclature, isomerism, carbocations, carbanions, free radicals, inductive & resonance effects.
  • Hydrocarbons: Alkanes, alkenes, alkynes; Markovnikov rule, acidity of alkynes, benzene aromaticity.
  • Haloalkanes & Haloarenes: \(S_N1\) and \(S_N2\) reaction mechanisms, Chloroform, Chlorobenzene.
  • Alcohols, Phenols & Ethers: Oxo-process, Fermentation, Williamson's synthesis.
  • Carbonyl Compounds & Carboxylic Acids: Aldehydes, Ketones, Aldol condensation, Cannizzaro reaction, Acid derivatives.
  • Nitrogen Compounds & Organometallics: Amines, Hoffmann separation, Aniline, Grignard reagents.
3. Inorganic Chemistry (10 / 7 Qs)

Chemistry of Non-metals (Hydrogen, Ozone, Nitrogen, Halogens, Sulphur); Chemistry of Metals (Metallurgy, Alkali/Alkaline earth metals, 3d transition metals, Copper, Iron, Silver, Complex ions); Bio-inorganic chemistry (Na-K pump, metal toxicity).

4. Applied Chemistry (3 Qs)

Industrial processes (Haber, Ostwald, Contact, Solvay); Polymers, drugs, dyes, pesticides, fertilizers; Environmental impact of chemical industries.

5. Analytical Chemistry (3 Qs)

Qualitative analysis (Acid/Basic radical tests); Separation techniques (Distillation, Chromatography, Crystallization); Titration types and indicator selections.

PHYSICS

Weightage: 50 Questions (MBBS/BDS) | 40 Questions (Other Groups)

50 / 40 MCQs
1. Mechanics (10 Qs / 8 Qs)

Vectors & Scalars; Kinematics (linear & projectile motion); Dynamics (Newton's laws, impulse, friction, work-energy theorem); Rotational dynamics (Moment of inertia, torque); Fluid statics & dynamics (Surface tension, Viscosity, Bernoulli's principle, Stokes law); Simple Harmonic Motion & Gravitation.

2. Heat & Thermodynamics (7 Qs / 6 Qs)

Thermometry; Thermal expansion; Calorimetry & latent heat; Ideal gas equation & Kinetic theory; First law of thermodynamics (Isothermal, Adiabatic, Isochoric, Isobaric processes); Second law (Heat engines, refrigerators, Entropy).

3. Waves & Optics (8 Qs / 6 Qs)

Wave motion; Velocity of sound; Stationary waves in pipes and strings; Doppler effect; Reflection & Refraction; Chromatic aberration; Interference (Young's double slit); Diffraction & Polarization (Brewster's law).

4. Electricity & Magnetism (9 Qs / 7 Qs)

Ohm's law & Joule's law; Kirchhoff's laws, Wheatstone bridge, Potentiometer; Thermoelectricity (Seebeck & Peltier); Alternating currents (LRC circuits); Magnetic fields (Biot-Savart, Ampere's law, Hall effect); Electromagnetic induction (Faraday's & Lenz's law, Transformers).

5. Electrostatics & Capacitors (4 Qs / 3 Qs)

Coulomb's law, Gauss's law, Electric potential & field intensity, Parallel plate capacitors, Energy density, Dielectrics.

6. Modern Physics (12 Qs / 10 Qs)

Nuclear physics (Binding energy, Fusion, Fission); Electrons & Photons (Photoelectric effect, Thomson & Millikan experiments); Bohr's theory & X-rays; Radioactivity & Carbon dating; Semiconductors & Logic Gates (AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR); Recent trends (Quarks, Higgs boson, Nanotechnology).

5. Sample Questions & Exam Pattern

Official sample MCQs as published by Medical Education Commission

OMR Single Best Response
OMR Bubble Sheet Instructions

Candidates must use the Black Gel Ink Pen provided at the examination center to mark their responses on the answer sheet. Bubble filling must be complete without overlapping or double marks.

Core Science Sample 1 Mark

Q1. Which one of the following drugs is approved by FDA for treatment of COVID-19?

A. Remdesivir
B. Chloroquine
C. Azithromycin
D. Amoxicillin
Correct Key: A (Remdesivir)
MAT: Verbal Reasoning 1 Mark

Q2. Rita goes 30 km towards North from a fixed point, then after turning to her right she goes 20 km. After this she goes 30 km after turning to her right. How far and in what direction is she from her starting point?

A. 10 km west
B. 15 km east
C. 20 km east
D. 30 km west
Correct Key: C (20 km east)

Explanation: North move (+30Y) canceled by South move (-30Y). Net displacement = 20 km towards East.

MAT: Numerical Reasoning 1 Mark

Q3. The length and breadth of a rectangle are increased by 15% and 25% respectively. What is the percentage increase in the area of the rectangle?

A. 20%
B. 30%
C. 40%
D. 44%
Correct Key: D (44%)

Formula: \(\% \text{ Increase} = x + y + \frac{x \times y}{100} = 15 + 25 + \frac{375}{100} = 43.75\% \approx 44\%\).

MAT: Logical Sequencing 1 Mark

Q4. Look at this series: 53, 53, 40, 40, 27, 27, ......... Which pair of numbers should come next?

A. 12
B. 14, 14
C. 27
D. 53
Correct Key: B (14, 14)

Explanation: The series repeats each number twice and decreases by 13: \(53-13=40\), \(40-13=27\), \(27-13=14\).

Frequently Asked Questions (MECEE-BL 2026)

Common queries regarding pass marks, eligibility, and negative marking

Is there negative marking in MECEE-BL examination?

Yes, as established in the Second Review, there is a deduction of 0.25 mark for each incorrect response. Unattempted questions carry zero penalty.

What is the qualifying cut-off score for merit list inclusion?

The qualifying criterion uses a Norm-Referenced System where candidates must score at or above the 50th percentile rank of that specific entrance examination session.

Can PCL Health Science diploma holders apply for MBBS/BDS?

Yes! PCL/Diploma holders in General Medicine, MLT, Dental Science, Ophthalmic, Pharmacy, Ayurveda, Radiography, or Physiotherapy who secured at least 50% aggregate AND obtained Grade 12 Science equivalence from NEB + Professional Council registration are fully eligible.
